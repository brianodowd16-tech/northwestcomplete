## 2. How a consultation works

1. **Tell us about the project**: room, fuel preference, chimney situation, timescale.
2. **Showroom visit**: see the fires burning and compare finishes. Bring room
   measurements (length, width, ceiling height), photos of the existing fireplace and
   chimney breast, and your BER certificate if you have one.
3. **Recommendation and quotation**: we confirm the right appliance, output (kW),
   flue or liner requirements and any hearth or surround work.
4. **Order**: online special offers can be paid by card and collected from the showroom.
   Larger projects are ordered through the team.
5. **Aftercare**: annual servicing, parts and maintenance products from our workshop.

## 3. Choosing a fuel

| Fuel | Good for | Things to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wood / multi-fuel stove | A real flame and strong heat, using an existing chimney or a new flue | Needs a sound, swept chimney or a twin-wall flue system. Burn only dry wood or approved smokeless fuels. |
| Gas (natural gas or LPG) | Instant, controllable heat with a realistic flame; remote and thermostatic control | By law, all gas work in Ireland must be done by a Registered Gas Installer (RGI). Balanced-flue models can be fitted where there is no chimney. |
| Electric fire / stove | Apartments, rooms with no chimney, low running effort | No flue or ventilation needed. Many models run the flame effect with or without heat. |
| Pellet stove | Automatic, programmable heat; clean burning | Needs a power supply and a flue, and pellet storage. Some models can heat radiators. |
| Oil stove | Homes off the gas network that already use oil | Needs an oil supply line and a flue. |

## 4. Sizing a stove or fire (heat output)

- A common rule of thumb: **room volume in m³ ÷ 14 ≈ kW needed** for an averagely
  insulated Irish room. Example: 5 m × 4 m × 2.4 m = 48 m³ → about 3.5 kW.
- Well-insulated or newer homes (A or B BER) usually need **less** output than this.
  Older, draughty rooms, high ceilings or large glazed areas may need more.
- Oversizing is a common mistake. A stove that is too powerful gets run slowly to
  avoid overheating the room, which burns dirtier, blackens the glass and tars the flue.
- Open-plan spaces and boiler stoves (which also heat radiators or water) are sized
  differently. Our team works these out on survey.

## 5. Chimneys, flues and ventilation

- **Existing chimney**: it should be inspected and swept before a new appliance goes
  in. Many older chimneys need a flexible stainless-steel liner for a stove to draw
  properly and safely.
- **No chimney**: options include a twin-wall flue system for a stove, a balanced-flue
  gas fire, or an electric fire with no flue at all.
- **Airtight and newer homes**: very airtight houses can't supply enough combustion air
  through the room. Ireland's Building Regulations (Technical Guidance Document Part J)
  generally call for a room-sealed appliance or a dedicated external air supply in these
  homes. Your installer confirms the requirement for your house.
- **Carbon monoxide**: Building Regulations require a carbon monoxide alarm when a new or
  replacement fuel-burning appliance is installed. We recommend one in any room with a
  stove or gas fire.
- **Hearths**: solid-fuel stoves need a non-combustible hearth of the required size and
  clearances to combustible materials, as set out in the appliance manual and Part J.

## 6. Fuel rules and good burning (Ireland)

- Since 2022, Ireland's Solid Fuel Regulations ban the sale of smoky (bituminous) coal
  nationwide and restrict the sale of wet wood in small quantities.
- Burn dry, seasoned or kiln-dried wood, ideally with a moisture content under 20%.
  Wet wood gives less heat, more smoke and blackened glass, and builds up tar in the
  chimney.
- Multi-fuel stoves can burn approved smokeless fuels. Never burn household waste,
  painted or treated timber.
- New solid-fuel stoves sold in the EU since 2022 must meet **Ecodesign** limits for
  efficiency and emissions. The team can confirm the Ecodesign rating of any model.

## 7. Servicing and maintenance

- Have the chimney swept at least once a year, ideally before the heating season.
  Heavily used stoves or ones burning wood may need it more often.
- Check the door rope seals each year. A worn seal lets in air, so the stove burns too
  fast and is hard to control. We replace seals for most makes.
- Cracked glass, warped baffles, burnt-out grates and cracked fire bricks should be
  replaced. Bring in the old part or a photo with the stove make and model.
- Gas fires should be serviced annually by an RGI.
- Clean stove glass when cold with a dedicated stove-glass cleaner. Persistent
  blackening usually means wet fuel or the stove being run too low.

## 8. Grants and running costs

- SEAI home energy grants mainly cover insulation, heat pumps, solar and similar
  upgrades. Standalone stoves and fires are generally not grant-funded. Check seai.ie
  for current schemes.
- Running costs depend on the fuel price, the appliance's efficiency and how it is used.
  Modern Ecodesign stoves are far more efficient (often 75–85%+) than an open fire,
  which loses most of its heat up the chimney.

## 10. What the assistant should and shouldn't do

- Explain options and help the customer prepare for a consultation. Keep answers
  short, practical and specific to Irish homes.
- Don't confirm stock, lead times, installation prices or quotations. Those come from
  the team: offer to arrange a consultation.
- Don't give instructions for gas, electrical or structural work. Explain that a
  Registered Gas Installer, registered electrical contractor or qualified installer
  must do it.
- If someone describes a smell of gas, carbon monoxide symptoms, a chimney fire or
  smoke coming into the room, tell them to stop using the appliance, ventilate, leave
  the house if needed, and call the emergency services (112 or 999). For a gas leak,
  call the Gas Networks Ireland 24-hour emergency line on 1800 20 50 50.
